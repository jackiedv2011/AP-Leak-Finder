Exporting from QuickBooks Online

Four reports, each exported to Excel or CSV. Fifteen minutes the first time, less after that.

What Reclaim reads

Reclaim works from four exports out of QuickBooks Online: your bills, the payments that went against them, your vendor list, and any credits. Nothing is connected to your account, and nothing is changed inside it. You export the files, you upload them, and you decide what to do with what comes back.

The four files do different jobs, and the comparison only works when all four are there. A duplicate payment is invisible in the bill file alone; an unused credit is invisible in the payment file alone.

Files

What to export

  • Bills and Applied Payments
  • Transaction List by Vendor
  • Vendor Contact List
  • Vendor Balance Detail

Purpose

What each one does

  • Bills give every invoice its vendor, number, date and amount
  • Payments show what actually left, and against which bill
  • Vendors let two spellings of one supplier be recognised
  • Credits show money already owed back to you

Reading time about four minutes · Export time about fifteen

Before you begin

What you need

A QuickBooks Online login with permission to run and export reports.

A date range in mind — two years is a good first pass.

Somewhere to put four files while you work.

Export permission is separate

In most systems, being able to see a report and being able to export it are two different permissions. If the export control is missing rather than greyed out, it is a permission question for whoever administers your account, not a problem with the report.

01

Open the Reports area

Sign in to QuickBooks Online and open Reports from the left-hand menu. Everything in this guide comes from that one screen, so you can leave it open for all four exports.

The search box at the top of the report list is the quickest way to get to a report. Type part of the name rather than hunting through the categories — the report names below are exact.

# the reports list
https://qbo.intuit.com/app/reports

One company at a time

If you run more than one company file, export each one separately. Reclaim matches bills to payments inside a single set of books, so mixing two companies in one upload will produce results that look like duplicates but are not.

02

Set the date range

Every report has a Report period dropdown at the top. Set it to the window you want Reclaim to look at, then select Run report.

Use the same range for all four exports. If the ranges differ, a payment can land in one file while the bill it paid sits outside another, and that gap reads like a mismatch when it is only a cropping problem.

How far back to go

Two years is a sensible first pass. Duplicate payments are often months apart, and credits can sit unused for a year or more before anyone notices them.

Accrual, not cash

Where a report offers an accounting method, choose Accrual. Cash basis hides bills that have been entered but not yet paid, which is exactly the population worth checking.

03

Export your bills and payments

Run Bills and Applied Payments. This report lists each bill with the payments applied against it, which is the single most useful file for finding an invoice that was paid more than once.

Select the export icon above the report and choose Export to Excel. CSV works too — Reclaim reads both.

04

Export the full transaction list

Run Transaction List by Vendor. This one catches everything the first report does not: bill payments recorded without a bill, expenses entered directly, checks, and card charges.

Before you export it, open Customize and make sure the columns include the transaction type, number, date, amount and memo. The memo field is often where a duplicate gives itself away.

Why two files

A bill paid twice through two different routes — once as a bill payment and once as a direct expense — only appears in both files together. Neither one shows it on its own.

05

Export your vendor list

Run Vendor Contact List and export it the same way. It is a small file and it does more work than it looks like it should.

Duplicate vendor records are one of the quietest causes of a duplicate payment: the same supplier under two spellings, each with its own open bills, neither one visible from the other. The contact list is what lets Reclaim spot that the two are the same company.

Include inactive vendors

Open Customize and set the vendor status to include inactive records. A vendor that was merged or deactivated last year can still be the reason two bills look unrelated.

06

Export credits and open balances

Run Vendor Balance Detail. It shows every open item per vendor, including vendor credits that were issued but never applied to a bill.

This is the file behind unused credits. A credit note sitting against a vendor you have since stopped using is money you already paid for and never took back.

07

Check the files before you upload

Open each file once. You are looking for three things, and they take about a minute between them.

First, that the header row is the real header and not a title line — QuickBooks sometimes puts the company name and the report period in the first few rows. Second, that amounts are numbers and not text. Third, that the file is not empty because the date range excluded everything.

Keep the originals

Upload the exports as they came out. Reclaim reads the original columns, and every result it reports points back at a row in the file you gave it. Edited files break that link.

Troubleshooting

I can't find a report by that name

Report names vary a little by QuickBooks Online plan. If Bills and Applied Payments is not in your list, Transaction List by Vendor filtered to bills and bill payments covers the same ground. Send us what you can see and we will tell you which one to use.

The export opens as one long column

That is a CSV being opened with the wrong delimiter. Re-export as Excel, or open the CSV through Data → From Text/CSV in Excel and set the delimiter to comma. Either way the file itself is fine.

Should I export as Excel or CSV?

Either. Excel keeps the column types, which saves a step, but Reclaim reads both. What matters more is that you do not re-save the file through another tool in between.

My date range covers two financial years

That is fine and often better. Duplicate payments cross year ends as easily as they cross months, and nothing in Reclaim depends on the range lining up with a reporting period.

Can Reclaim connect to QuickBooks directly instead?

Not today. Reclaim works from files you export and upload, which is deliberate: nothing is connected to your books, nothing is changed in them, and you can see exactly what you handed over.
