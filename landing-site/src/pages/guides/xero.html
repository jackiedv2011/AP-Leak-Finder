Exporting from Xero

Three reports and one contact export. Xero puts most of what Reclaim needs in the payables reports.

What Reclaim reads

Reclaim works from four exports out of Xero: your bills, the payments that went against them, your vendor list, and any credits. Nothing is connected to your account, and nothing is changed inside it. You export the files, you upload them, and you decide what to do with what comes back.

The four files do different jobs, and the comparison only works when all four are there. A duplicate payment is invisible in the bill file alone; an unused credit is invisible in the payment file alone.

Files

What to export

  • Payable Invoice Detail
  • Account Transactions
  • Supplier contact export
  • Aged Payables Detail

Purpose

What each one does

  • Bills give every invoice its vendor, number, date and amount
  • Payments show what actually left, and against which bill
  • Vendors let two spellings of one supplier be recognised
  • Credits show money already owed back to you

Reading time about four minutes · Export time about fifteen

Before you begin

What you need

A Xero login with permission to run and export reports.

A date range in mind — two years is a good first pass.

Somewhere to put four files while you work.

Export permission is separate

In most systems, being able to see a report and being able to export it are two different permissions. If the export control is missing rather than greyed out, it is a permission question for whoever administers your account, not a problem with the report.

01

Open Reports

Sign in to Xero and go to Accounting, then Reports. Three of the four exports in this guide start here; the fourth comes from your contact list.

If you have starred reports before, the ones below may already be on your dashboard. Otherwise use the search box at the top of the reports page.

# the reports page
https://go.xero.com/app/reports

One organisation at a time

Xero keeps each organisation separate, and so should the upload. Exporting two organisations and uploading them together will make ordinary payments in one look like duplicates of the other.

02

Set the date range

Each report has a date selector at the top. Set the range you want, then select Update to re-run the report before exporting it.

Use the same range everywhere. A payment inside the window whose bill sits outside it will look unmatched, which is a cropping artefact rather than a finding.

Two years is a good start

Credits in particular can sit unapplied for a long time. A short window will find recent duplicates and miss the older money.

Check the basis

Where a Xero report offers a cash or accrual view, take the accrual one. Bills that are entered but unpaid are part of what Reclaim compares against.

03

Export payable invoices

Run Payable Invoice Detail. It lists supplier bills line by line with their status, which is the base file for duplicate bills and for bills paid twice.

Select Export at the top of the report and choose Excel. Xero also offers PDF and Google Sheets; take the Excel file, because PDF loses the column structure Reclaim reads.

04

Export account transactions

Run Account Transactions for your bank and credit card accounts. This is the payments side: what actually left the account, and when.

Payable Invoice Detail tells you what was owed. Account Transactions tells you what was paid. A duplicate payment is visible where those two disagree, and neither report shows it alone.

Include every account money leaves from

Operating account, card accounts, and any account used for one-off supplier payments. A bill paid once from the main account and once from a card is exactly the case that gets missed.

05

Export your supplier list

Go to Contacts, open your suppliers, and use the export option to download them as CSV.

This file is small and it matters more than its size suggests. The same supplier entered twice — once with a trading name, once with the registered name — produces two sets of bills that never get compared. The contact export is how Reclaim works out that they are one company.

Include archived contacts

A supplier archived after a merge still has history attached. Leaving archived contacts out is one of the easier ways to hide a duplicate.

06

Export credits and open items

Run Aged Payables Detail. It shows what is outstanding per supplier, including credit notes that were raised and never applied.

An unapplied credit note is money you are owed sitting quietly on the supplier record. It does not chase itself, and it rarely shows up in a payment run.

07

Check the files before you upload

Open each export once before you send it. There are three things worth a glance.

That the first row is the column header and not the organisation name, which Xero sometimes places above it. That amounts came through as numbers. And that the file has rows in it at all, rather than an empty range you did not notice.

Send them as they came out

Do not tidy the files. Reclaim points every result at a row in the export you gave it, and a re-saved or reformatted file breaks that trail.

Troubleshooting

My report doesn't have an Export button

Older report versions in Xero export from a different menu, and some custom reports need to be saved first. If you cannot find it, send us a screenshot of the report header and we will point at the right control.

Should I use Excel, CSV or Google Sheets?

Excel or CSV. Reclaim reads both. Avoid PDF — it looks the same to a person and loses the column structure entirely.

Do I need tracking categories?

No. Reclaim matches on supplier, amount, reference and date. Tracking categories are useful to you when you review a result, but nothing depends on them.

Can I upload the same range twice?

Yes, and it does no harm. Reclaim compares the records inside one upload; running a wider range later simply gives it more to compare.

Can Reclaim connect to Xero directly instead?

Not today. Reclaim works from files you export and upload. Nothing connects to your organisation, nothing is changed inside it, and you can see exactly what you handed over.
