Reclaim was built on one fact:

Money that leaves
by mistake
leaves a record.

We build one thing
and build it properly.

Reclaim reads the records your accounting system already holds — bills, payments, vendors, credits — and shows where the same invoice went out twice, where a payment came to more than the bill, and where a credit was issued and never used. Then, with your approval, it asks the vendor for the money back and follows the case until it has actually arrived.

What we believe

Five things we hold to, and build everything else from.

  • 01

    Mistakes in accounts payable are normal, not rare.

    APQC's benchmark of 1,686 organizations puts duplicate or erroneous payments at 1.5% of all disbursements at the median.

  • 02

    The evidence is already sitting in your own records.

    Nothing has to be reconstructed. The bill, the payment and the credit are already in the books your system keeps today.

  • 03

    A number without the row behind it is only a guess.

    Every result names the records behind it and the fields that matched. If we can't point at it, we don't claim it, and a promised refund isn't money back until it arrives.

  • 04

    Access should be the least anyone asks for.

    Reclaim reads your books through a read-only connection you can revoke, or from files you upload. It can't change your books or move money, and AI only ever sees what one task needs.

  • 05

    The decision always stays with you, start to finish.

    Nothing goes to a vendor until you approve it, and disputes come back to you. What to raise, what to write off and what to leave alone is your call throughout.

Accounts payable is where a company's money actually leaves, and it is also where the work is most repetitive. Nearly one invoice in five needs a person to step in.

Ardent Partners puts the average invoice exception rate at 18.4% and the average cost of processing a single invoice at $9.84. APQC's benchmark of 1,686 organizations puts duplicate or erroneous payments at 1.5% of all disbursements at the median. Those are rates, not accidents.

They repeat every year, and the records that would show them are already sitting in the books. What has been missing is a plain way to read those records, point at the rows, and see the money through to the point where it is actually back.

That is what we build.

We would rather show you three findings you can check than thirty you have to believe. A number on a screen means nothing until you can open the row behind it, see the two records side by side, and decide for yourself whether it is worth chasing. That is the only kind of number worth having.

What we're building

Reclaim starts with four kinds of money: the same payment made twice, the same bill paid twice, a payment larger than its bill, and a vendor credit issued and never used. For each one it finds the case, proves it from your records, runs the recovery once you approve it, and checks that the money actually came back.

Next, Reclaim takes on more of the routine work: reading vendor replies, gathering evidence, and suggesting the next step. After that, it reads more than your ledger: supplier statements, your AP inbox and your bank, so it can find credits your books never recorded and keep watching for new mistakes.

What won't change is the part we care about most: every result keeps pointing at the records it came from.
