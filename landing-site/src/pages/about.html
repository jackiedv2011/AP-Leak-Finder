Reclaim was built on one fact:

Money that leaves
by mistake
leaves a record.

We build one thing
and build it properly.

Reclaim reads the exports your accounting system already produces — bills, payments, vendors, credits — and shows where the same invoice went out twice, where an amount doesn't match what was agreed, and where a credit was issued and never used.

What we believe

Five things we hold to, and build everything else from.

  • 01

    Mistakes in accounts payable are normal, not rare.

    APQC's benchmark of 1,686 organizations puts duplicate or erroneous payments at 1.5% of all disbursements at the median.

  • 02

    The evidence is already sitting in your own records.

    Nothing has to be reconstructed. The bill, the payment and the credit are already in exports your system produces today.

  • 03

    A number without the row behind it is only a guess.

    Every result names the file, the row and the fields that matched. If we can't point at it, we don't claim it.

  • 04

    Access should be the last thing anyone asks for.

    Reclaim works from files you choose to upload. No login to your accounting system, no bank connection, nothing that moves money.

  • 05

    The decision always stays with you, start to finish.

    We find and we show. What to raise, what to write off and what to leave alone is your call throughout.

Accounts payable is where a company's money actually leaves, and it is also where the work is most repetitive. Nearly one invoice in five needs a person to step in.

Ardent Partners puts the average invoice exception rate at 18.4% and the average cost of processing a single invoice at $9.84. APQC's benchmark of 1,686 organizations puts duplicate or erroneous payments at 1.5% of all disbursements at the median. Those are rates, not accidents.

They repeat every year, and the records that would show them are already exported every month. What has been missing is a plain way to read those files and point at the rows.

That is what we build.

We would rather show you three findings you can check than thirty you have to believe. A number on a screen means nothing until you can open the row behind it, see the two records side by side, and decide for yourself whether it is worth chasing. That is the only kind of number worth having.

What we're building

Reclaim looks for five things: the same bill entered twice, the same bill paid twice, an amount that doesn't match what was agreed, a payment that doesn't match its bill, and a credit issued and never used.

Next is making that check easy to repeat, so a month of records can be run the way you run a reconciliation.

What won't change is the part we care about most: every result keeps pointing at the row it came from.
