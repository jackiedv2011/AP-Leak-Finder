# Verified research (primary sources, checked 2026-09-17)

## APQC — Open Standards Benchmarking, measure 105905
URL: https://www.apqc.org/resources/benchmarking/open-standards-benchmarking/measures/percentage-total-annual-number
- "Percentage of total annual number of disbursements processed which are duplicate or erroneous payments"
- Total sample size: 1,686 (all companies). Median: 1.5%. (25th/75th hidden behind login)
- Unit: count of disbursements, not dollar value. "A disbursement is a single remittance to a supplier..."

## ACFE — Occupational Fraud 2026: A Report to the Nations (May 2026)
URL: https://www.acfe.com/-/media/files/acfe/pdfs/rttn/2026/2026-report-to-the-nations.pdf  (landing: https://www.acfe.com/fraud-resources/report-to-the-nations)
- 2,402 cases, 143 countries and territories, total losses > $3.4 billion
- Median loss per case $104,000; average $1,457,000
- CFEs estimate organizations lose 5% of revenue to fraud each year
- Typical case lasted 12 months before detection
- 43% of frauds detected by tips; 55% of tips from employees, 21% customers, 11% vendors
- Billing schemes: 508 cases, 21% of cases, median loss $90,000 (Fig. 5, p15; key findings p4)
- Check and payment tampering: 243 cases, 10%, median loss $114,000
- Billing schemes median duration 14 months (p17)
- Billing schemes in orgs <100 employees: 28% of cases vs 18% in 100+ (Fig. 24, p34)
- Frauds caught in first 6 months: median loss $40,000; delaying another 1–6 months more than doubled it ($100,000) (p17)
- Glossary: billing scheme = invoices for fictitious goods or services, inflated invoices, or invoices for personal purchases
- More than half of all cases involved a lack of internal controls or an override of existing controls (ACFE blog, key findings)

## AFP — 2026 Payments Fraud and Control Survey (press release April 14, 2026)
URL: https://www.financialprofessionals.org/about/learn-more/press-releases/Details/over-75-percent-of-us-firms-experienced-payments-fraud-in-2025-while-ai-adoption-for-fraud-mitigation-lags

- Financial losses reported by 48% of orgs with revenue under $1B, 66% over $1B

## Additional detail (checked 2026-09-17, same primary sources)

### APQC measure 105905 page
- Measure category: Process Efficiency. "Key performance indicator: No". Scope: Cross Industry (PCF 8.0), process 9.6.1 (verify AP pay file with PO/vendor master, audit invoices and key data in AP system, research/resolve payable exceptions, process payments, etc.)
- Units: percent; 25th/75th percentiles require login

### ACFE 2026 (PDF text)
- Asset misappropriation 90% of cases, $100,000 median; financial statement fraud 6%, $1,000,000; corruption 45%, $150,000
- Most common asset misappropriation: theft of noncash assets 23%; billing 21%. Most costly: check and payment tampering $114,000; billing $90,000
- Detection: tip 43%, internal audit 15%, management review 13%, document examination 5%, account reconciliation 5%, by accident 4%, automated transaction/data monitoring 3%, external audit 2%
- Active detection methods: fraud "can be uncovered up to four times faster" than passive methods
- Caught in first six months: median $40,000, one-third of cases; another 1-6 months more than doubled it ($100,000); >5 years (5% of cases): $1.115M
- Median durations: billing 14 months; check and payment tampering 13 months; overall 12 months
- Size: <100 employees median loss $126,000 (highest), 22% of cases; 1,000-9,999 lowest ($100,000)
- Billing schemes 28% of cases at <100 employees vs 18% at 100+; billing, skimming, check and payment tampering had the largest small-vs-large differences
- Controls: all 18 associated with lower losses and faster detection. Proactive data monitoring/analysis: in place at 49% of victims; median loss $70,000 with vs $150,000 without (53% lower); duration 9 vs 16 months (44% faster). Management review: 71%; $84,000 vs $186,000 (55%); 10 vs 18 months. Surprise audits: 44%; $74,000 vs $149,000 (50%); 8 vs 16 months (50%)
- Presence of a control reduced losses by 37% on average; when lack of controls was the primary factor, median losses were 43% higher
- Hotlines: 85% of larger organizations vs one-quarter of small businesses
- 80% of victim organizations modified controls after the fraud

### FBI IC3 2025 (PDF text)
- Almost 3,000 complaints per day on average
- Investment fraud largest loss category, followed by BEC and tech support scams
- BEC definition: targets businesses or individuals working with suppliers and/or regularly performing wire transfers; compromises email and other communication through social engineering or intrusion to conduct unauthorized transfers
- Businesses reported losses over $30 million to BEC scams involving AI in 2025

### GAO-26-108694 page + press release (Apr 27, 2026)
- Improper payments: "should not have been made or were made in incorrect amounts"
- FY2025 about $186B, 64 programs, 15 agencies; +$24B from FY2024 ($162B)
- About $153B (about 82%) overpayments
- 73% concentrated in five program areas: Medicare, Medicaid, EITC, SNAP, Shuttered Venue Operators Grant
- 19 programs reported rates of at least 10%; six above 25%
- Increase largely from programs newly reporting estimates plus higher Medicaid improper payments
- Estimates do not represent the full extent (e.g., TANF not included); cumulative since FY2003 about $3T, "actual amount may be much higher"
- IGs: half of 24 agencies fully complied with PIIA in FY2024
- GAO recommendations include designating new programs paying >$100M a year as susceptible, and internal control plans for emergency funding

### AFP 2026 press release (April 14, 2026)
- 17% of organizations use AI to combat payments fraud
- BEC at 74% was a significant increase from 2023 and 2024
- Treasury most likely to discover attempted fraud (83%) and actual fraud (55%)
- Smaller firms face fraud less often but are much more likely to absorb the full loss

### Ardent Partners (Jan 22, 2026, Andrew Bartolini; report "The State of ePayables 2025: AP's Unfinished Journey")
- Exceptions "typically the biggest single reason" benchmarks are not lower; supplier time (21.9%) "in no small part caused by exceptions"
- Best-in-Class = the 20% of enterprises with lowest average invoice processing costs and shortest cycle times
- Best-in-Class: 1.4x more suppliers e-invoicing, more than 1.8x as many invoices straight-through, roughly half as much time on supplier inquiries
- 39% of AP leaders say manual, low-value work stunts their potential
